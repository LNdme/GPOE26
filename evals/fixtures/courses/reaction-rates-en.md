# Rates of reaction

## What is the rate of a reaction?

The rate of a reaction tells us how fast reactants are turned into products. It can be measured by following how quickly a product forms (for example, the volume of gas collected each minute) or how quickly a reactant is used up.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{\text{amount of product formed}}{\text{time taken}}$$

## Collision theory

Particles can only react when they collide with enough energy. The minimum energy needed is called the **activation energy**. Anything that makes successful collisions more frequent increases the rate.

## Factors that affect the rate

- **Temperature**: particles move faster, collide more often and with more energy.
- **Concentration** (or pressure for gases): more particles in the same volume, so more frequent collisions.
- **Surface area**: powdering a solid exposes more particles, so the reaction is faster.
- **Catalyst**: see below.

## Catalysts

A **catalyst** is a substance that speeds up a reaction without being used up. It provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy. Enzymes are biological catalysts. In this course, the example used is manganese(IV) oxide, which catalyses the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.

## Practical: marble chips and acid

Marble chips (calcium carbonate) react with hydrochloric acid to give carbon dioxide gas. Using smaller chips with the same mass makes the gas come off faster, because the surface area is larger.
